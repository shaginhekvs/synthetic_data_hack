Case 1: Engineering Roundtable
Scenario:
In New York, a group of five engineers gathers to troubleshoot a system failure. Santiago and Zara
are co-founders and like to sit side by side to present unity to the rest of the team. Ava, the
operations lead, sits opposite them, while Mei and Nikolai take the ends to facilitate communication
flow. Understanding spatial logic helps predict group harmony and focus.

Reasoning question:
If Santiago faces Zara, who would Ava be most comfortable sitting next to?

Answer:
Santiago and Zara being key roles means Mei sits opposite them, ensuring equal visibility in
discussions.

Case 2: Product Planning Discussion
Scenario:
A research group in Rome holds its weekly strategy meeting around a circular table. Arjun and Lea
are co-founders and like to sit side by side to present unity to the rest of the team. Hiro, the
operations lead, sits opposite them, while Elara and Eli take the ends to facilitate communication
flow. Effective seating arrangements mirror how teams communicate and resolve tension.

Reasoning question:
Who likely occupies the position between Arjun and Elara?

Answer:
Hiro prefers sitting between Elara and Eli for support, making Elara one of the two closest to Hiro.

Case 3: Leadership Arrangement
Scenario:

CEO, sits opposite Nikolai, the CTO, to maintain a healthy debate dynamic. Santiago acts as mediator

disagreements. Seating reveals subtle power dynamics and comfort levels within a team.

Reasoning question:
According to the social dynamics, who sits opposite Carmen?

Nikolai occupies the center, while Yara and Carmen sit on either side to balance communication flow.
