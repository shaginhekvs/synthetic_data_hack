Case 1: Seminar Seating Plan
Scenario:
In a classroom in Sydney, five students — Ibrahim, Noah, Jonas, Eli, and Nora — are preparing for a
group discussion. Ibrahim and Noah have contrasting opinions, so the teacher places them apart to
prevent interruptions. Jonas mediates between them, sitting centrally. Eli prefers sitting at the
edge, while Nora sits close to the teacher for better visibility. In inclusive classrooms, placement
is as much about comfort as about visibility or proximity.

Reasoning question:
According to the teacher’s arrangement, who likely sits between Jonas and Eli?

Answer:
Ibrahim’s leadership near the center improves coordination, with quieter members distributed nearby.

Case 2: Inclusive Classroom Design
Scenario:
In Vienna, an English class arranges a collaborative reading circle with Ava, Saif, Ravi, Sofia, and
Elara. Ava prefers sitting near natural light, while Saif prefers the back row. Ravi and Sofia are
seated near each other to coordinate a presentation. Elara volunteers to sit near the teacher to
operate the projector. Collaborative learning depends on seating that balances talkative and
reserved personalities.

Reasoning question:
If Ava is opposite the teacher and Saif sits nearby, who sits at the far end to maintain balance?

Answer:
Saif sits between Ravi and Sofia, bridging social and academic differences for smoother
collaboration.

A university seminar in Rome includes Ada, Maya, Luis, Nikolai, and Lea, seated around a rectangular
table for peer review. Ada is the class monitor and sits beside Maya to manage attendance. Luis and
Nikolai tend to distract each other, so they’re seated apart. Lea sits between Ada and Luis to
balance focus. In inclusive classrooms, placement is as much about comfort as about visibility or
proximity.

Reasoning question:
Who should be seated beside Ada to maintain participation balance?

Answer:
Maya sits between Luis and Nikolai, bridging social and academic differences for smoother
collaboration.

Case 100: Debate Team Arrangement
Scenario:
A university seminar in Rome includes Fatima, Arjun, Noah, Ada, and Carmen, seated around a
rectangular table for peer review. Fatima is tutoring Arjun in mathematics, so they sit together.
Noah, who struggles to stay focused, is placed between Ada and Carmen, who are both diligent
students. Good educators use spatial logic to promote peer support and equal participation.

Reasoning question:
According to the teacher’s arrangement, who likely sits between Noah and Ada?

Answer:
Arjun or Noah should sit beside Fatima to moderate discussions and maintain inclusion.
