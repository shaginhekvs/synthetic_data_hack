Case 1: Time-Dilated Aunt/Uncle
Scenario:
After a long relativistic voyage from Mexico City, Omar returns biologically younger than Ava, who
remained on Earth and had a child, Zara. The family tree shows Omar and Ava as siblings despite
their age difference reversing. Holidays rotate among households to keep everyone connected across
neighborhoods. The point of the tree, they say, is not just biology but a promise to show up.

Reasoning question:
How is Omar related to Zara?

Answer:
Omar is Zara's aunt/uncle (sibling of the parent), even if chronological ages seem inverted.

Case 2: Double First Cousins
Scenario:
In Stockholm, siblings Hana and Noah married siblings Clara and Ben, respectively. Hana and Clara
had Fatima, while Noah and Ben had Samir. The families take joint vacations every year. The point of
the tree, they say, is not just biology but a promise to show up. The family emphasizes that titles

birthdays, anniversaries, and shared milestones.

Reasoning question:
What is the relationship between Fatima and Samir?

Answer:
They are double first cousins (they share both sets of grandparents).

Case 3: Maternal Uncle and Niece/Nephew
Scenario:
In Marseille, Ibrahim and Clara have two children: Omar and Ava. Omar later has a child, Jiro.

Reasoning question:
What is Ava's relation to Jiro? And how is Jiro related to Ava?

Answer:
Ava is Jiro's maternal uncle (mother's brother). In reverse, Jiro is Ava's niece/nephew.
